# Eddie Williams — Chief Marketing Officer
### The Discipline Behind the Message

Eddie Williams believes that if something is worth doing, it's worth doing the right way.

That standard was instilled in him early by his mother, Lois Williams, a Civil Rights Activist, whose example helped shape Eddie's approach to business, technology, and life: be prepared, pay attention to the details, understand what you're doing, and don't put your name behind something unless you're willing to do it right.

Eddie brings that philosophy to his role as our Chief Marketing Officer.

With an MBA in Business Administration from Walsh College and a bachelor's degree from Grand Valley State University, Eddie combines formal business education with years of practical experience in technology, analysis, testing, and reporting. His professional journey has taken him from working in a college bookstore to building a career with the largest non-profit-ish Blue Cross Blue Shield of Michigan, where he developed his niche by learning the technology behind the business and using testing, analysis, and reporting to understand whether systems are actually delivering what they were designed to accomplish.

That experience gives Eddie a different perspective on marketing.

For him, marketing isn't just about creating something that looks good or sounds good. The message has to work. Who are we trying to reach? What are we trying to communicate? How do we know they received it? What does the data tell us? And most importantly—did it produce the result we intended?

Eddie approaches a marketing campaign much like he approaches technology: understand it, implement it, test it, measure it, report on it, and make it better.

As our Chief Marketing Officer, Eddie helps ensure that the vision doesn't get lost between what we build and what the public sees. He brings structure and accountability to our storytelling while making sure our message represents who we are and reaches the people who need to hear it.

Because Eddie understands that good marketing gets people's attention—but disciplined marketing earns their trust and produces results.
